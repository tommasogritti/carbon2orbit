# Architecture

How Carbon to Orbit is put together, for people and agents about to change it. What to build and why lives in [PROJECT_SPEC.md](PROJECT_SPEC.md); this file is how the code works today. To add or change a level, follow [the add-level skill](../.claude/skills/add-level/SKILL.md).

## The shape of it

A static, client-only web game: Vite + TypeScript + Phaser 3, no server and no build-time content pipeline. Three ideas explain most of the code.

1. **A level is data.** Every node on the map is a `LevelDef` in `src/content.ts`: story text, sources, map position, and `mechanic` + `params`. The mechanic names a scene; the params tell that scene what to be.
2. **A mechanic is a scene.** Each kind of play is one file in `src/scenes/`, a Phaser scene that receives `{ def }`, plays it from `def.params`, and ends by calling `finish()`. Scenes know nothing about the map or each other.
3. **All art and sound are code.** Textures are drawn with the Canvas 2D API at boot (`src/art.ts`, `src/backdrops.ts`); sound effects and music are synthesised with Web Audio (`src/audio.ts`). The repository ships no sprite sheets and no audio files, so a contributor needs only a text editor.

```
index.html ─ src/main.ts ─ Boot ─┬─ Title ── Map ──(Enter on a node)── intro card ── <mechanic scene> ── finish() ── result card ─┐
                                 │            ▲                                                                                  │
                                 │            └──────────────────────────────── back to the map ─────────────────────────────────┘
                                 └─ ?level=<id> jumps straight to that level's scene
```

**Launch control in XG.** `Launch.ts` keeps the rules, the physics, the keys and the sound; in XG graphics, for a guided vehicle at a site listed in `SITES_3D`, it also makes a `Launch3D` (`src/scenes/launch3d.ts`). That reads the scene's state on every frame, hands the engine a `Frame` with a `launch` part (stage one's place, the fairing, the exhaust, the pad, the site, a break-up), and redraws the consoles on its own canvas from the scene's own texts, `rows()`, the reference trajectory and the trail. The shader's launch mode (`uMode` 1) adds the whole Electron stack (`mapStack`), the pad's structures (`mapPad`), each site's coast, laid out from the real geography in a few shapes, as a height field (`coast`, `landH`, `shadeLand`), the mission patch as a decal on the fairing (`Engine.setPatch`), the exhaust, the steam at ignition and a break-up as volumes (`plume`, `padSmoke`, `boom`), and the floodlights and the flame as lights (`lampsE`). The kick stage is drawn too (`kickFrame`, the shader's `uKick`), with its orbit plot, plan and heading dial redrawn from the scene's state; the loss-of-telemetry card is the scene's own 2D screen, and `Launch3D` hides itself for it. Ātea-1 (unguided) plays as before.

**Orbit operations in XG.** The same arrangement for `orbit-ops`: `scenes/orbit3d.ts` reads `OrbitOpsScene` each frame (its orbit, its capsule, and `ui`, what the map shows) and hands the renderer one of three pictures: the spacecraft in orbit (the launch mode's kick stage with payload kind 5 and solar wings), the capsule's fall and its landing (shader mode 2: a capsule over site 3, the outback). The scene asks it two things: `rate`, how quickly to step the capsule's fall, and `land()`, how long to hold the result while the landing is shown.

A level can also be told in several acts: `params.then = { mechanic, params, intro?, how? }` hands a successful act to the next scene, and nothing is saved until the last act ends.

## Conventions every scene follows

- **Units.** The world is 320 × 180 units, rendered at 1280 × 720 (`D = 4` texture pixels per unit, `S = 1 / D`). Call `cam(this)` for a single-screen scene. Draw textures with `tex(scene, key, w, h, fn)` in units; place them with `img()`; write text with `T()`.
- **Scrolling scenes** zoom the camera by `D`, so a HUD object pinned with scroll factor 0 must be wrapped in `fix()`.
- **Input** comes from `held(key)` and `just(key)` in `core.ts`, never from Phaser's keyboard plugin. Keys are `left right up down a b` (arrows or WASD, Space or Z, Enter or X), which is also what the touch pad sends.
- **Time** comes from the `update(time, delta)` delta or scene timers, never from the wall clock: the controls panel pauses and resumes scenes at any moment, and the player can run a level at ×0.5 to ×4.
- **Ending.** Call `finish(scene, def, { success, stars, title, text, egg?, secret? })` exactly once. It saves, shows the result card with "what really happened", and returns to the map.
- **Controls panel.** Give the scene a `help()` method returning `{ title, rows: [keys, does][], note? }`, reflecting the level and the phase. Simple scenes can use an entry in `CONTROLS` in `core.ts` instead.
- **Hints (optional).** A hard puzzle can give the scene a `hint()` method returning the next hint for where the player is, `{ title, text, cost? }`, or `undefined` when none is left; it may charge for each hint in the level's own currency (the flight 13 investigation charges a day, the slot finder half an hour of clock). The 💡 button and `I` appear only in scenes that have one. Every level also gets the ⌂ button and `Esc`, which ask, then return to the map without saving the run.
- **A bot.** When the page has `?auto=1`, the scene plays itself to a three-star finish. This is how a level is shown to be winnable; `&fast=6` runs it six times faster.
- **Starting a scene.** Always pass data to `scene.start(key, data)`. Phaser reuses the previous data when none is given.

## The hi-fi layer

Any level can opt into a cinematic look without changing how it plays. `going-public` was the first: Long Beach at first light through a glass wall, with every object and word the plain backdrop had. There is one engine (`src/hifi.ts`) for every kind of scene; a scene uses the parts that fit it.

- **Opting in.** The scene names a look, the same way it picks its plain art. A platform theme: `hifi: '<name>'` in `THEMES` (`Platform.ts`). Launch control: by site, in `SITE_LOOK` (`Launch.ts`), or by level in `LEVEL_LOOK` where flights from one site each have their own light (Launch Complex 1: `src/looks/lc1.ts` is one site drawing and a `Day` of sun, sky and colour per flight). The hot-fire stand: `LOOK` in `Hotfire.ts`; the fault finder: `LOOK` in `FaultFinder.ts`; the two counts: `LOOK`, by theme, in `DualCount.ts`; the countdown race: `LOOK` in `CountdownRace.ts`; the edit bay and the slot finder: `hifi: '<name>'` in their `THEMES` (`EditBay.ts`, `SlotFinder.ts`); the hat trick: `hifi: { walk, table }` in its `THEMES` (`HatTrick.ts`), one look per act; the fit-out: `hifi: { lift, trial }` in its `THEMES` (`FitOut.ts`), one look per act; the arc lab: `LOOK` in `ArcLab.ts`. A ride theme: `hifi: '<name>'` in `THEMES` (`Ride.ts`). A look is an entry in `LOOKS` (`src/hifiLooks.ts`), written in its own file in `src/looks/` (`listing`, the first, is still in `hifiLooks.ts`): its layers or redrawn textures, tile set, lights, colour grade, and its own moving parts. A scene without a look renders exactly as before.
- **What every scene calls.** `hifi(scene, look, world?)` first in `create()`; it returns nothing when there is no look or the player chose standard graphics, so every later call is `hf?.…`. Last, `ready({...})`. The layer steps itself on the scene's `postupdate` event and cleans up on `shutdown`.

- **The HUD stays out of the post pass** on a camera of its own. A scene that already has a HUD camera (the map) says `ownHud` in its `world`, and the layer adds none. A scrolling scene's HUD is found by scroll factor 0. A single-screen scene has no scroll factors, so it names its HUD: `ready({ hud: [...] })`, and `lateHud: true` when whatever it adds later is HUD too.
- **Scene state.** `hf.set({...})` hands the look the numbers it animates from (altitude, throttle, chamber pressure); the look reads `hf.state` in `step()`. This keeps looks out of the scene's fields. A scene with no hero sprite gives the lead's place as `x`, `y`, and the look's `heroLight` follows that. The looks that are out in space share their sky and their Earth (`deepSky()`, `earthLit()`, `earthShade()`, `clouds` in `src/looks/cislunar.ts`). What every launch look shares on the way up (the shader plume and the sheen of the day's light on the vehicle, pad dust, glitter, lens, the sun's shafts, the pressure wave at ignition and the shake at lift-off and Max-Q, the sky going black, stars, the Earth's edge) is `src/looks/flight.ts`: `spaceTextures()`, `SPACE`, `ascentLive()`, `ascentStep()`. The review room in the dark, with a housing on its wall for whatever displays a scene has, is `roomFor()` in `src/looks/review.ts` (the fault finder and the arc lab). What a half-size pad picture on a console does round the clock (two skies crossing over with the scene's daylight, sunrise and moonrise in frame, floodlights after dark, a lift-off that lights the pad) is `src/looks/padview.ts`: `padTextures()`, `padView()`, `padStep()`. `hf.heat(x, y, r, amount)` bends the picture over a flame; `hf.power(i, k)` turns one of the look's lights up or down.
- **Art.** Still drawn in code, lazily, the first time a level uses the look (`Look.build`). `texAt()` draws a texture at any resolution: far layers are drawn coarse on purpose, which reads as depth of field. To keep every object and word, a look redraws the plain backdrop with the plain backdrop's own function, in parts: `parts()` (`art.ts`) lets one function draw only its shell, its things, or what gives off light, in the same order as before (`workshop`, `listing`, `factory`, `mahiaFar`, `rideFar`, `drawSite`, `drawWallops`, `drawStand`, `review`, `drawMap`, `roomQ2`, `roomQ1`, `worldmap`, `ameliaYard`, `ameliaBasin`; `planet` and `mars` take the direction of their light instead). A look may also work a texture out point by point where no brush would do: the world map (`src/looks/map.ts`) takes the plain map's coastlines and gives every point of land a height, a climate, a colour and the shade of its slope, so it reads as a satellite picture with no photograph in it (about 0.2 s, once). `relight()` then puts a drawing in the dark and brings it back under each lamp; `sheet()` is a scratch canvas; `mirrorStrip()` makes a floor reflection. A look keeps its glass and its room on separate layers so light shafts fall between them and never wash out text.
- **Lighting.** Phaser's Light2D pipeline on the ground, the hero, enemies and props, when the look gives an `ambient`. Normal maps are generated: `bevelNormals()` from a texture's outline, `paintNormals()` by hand for tiles. Lights that belong to a backdrop layer give that layer's width (`w`) and ride with it. `Look.flares` gives a light to the scene's own things by texture (a welding arm's arc); `Look.deco` lights the standing props. A look may move its own lights in `step()` by changing their `x` (the sun patches crossing Māhia). Phaser lights ten at a time, the nearest to the camera: keep to that on any one screen.
- **The hero** of a platform level is drawn by a stand-in image that copies the physics sprite each step and adds squash, stretch, lean and dust. Enemies get a shadow, a floor reflection (`Look.mirror`) and a rocking walk by rotation only. Nothing here may scale or move a physics body: that is what keeps the bot's run identical.
- **Post pass.** One `PostFXPipeline` on the play camera: thresholded bloom at half size, split-tone grade, vignette, grain, a little lens fringing, and heat shimmer where the look asks for it. A look can ask for more, and none of it runs or shows unless it does:

  | Call | What it does |
  | --- | --- |
  | `hf.rays(x, y, amt, tint?, size?, reach?)` | Shafts of light from a point in the world (the sun, a plume in the dark). Whatever stands close before the light cuts into them and the light wraps round its edges. `size` is how big the light itself is, in units. In a scrolling scene the sun rides on a far layer: `hf.wx(w, x)` gives where a point of the layer `w` wide is in the world now. |
  | `Grade.streak`, `hf.streak(k)` | The sideways streak of an anamorphic lens through small bright things. It belongs to dark scenes: against a bright sky it only lights up the horizon, and a broad bright thing (the Earth, a lit wall, a row of windows) is smeared by it. Keep it low at rest and raise it for the moment that earns it (a burn, speed). |
  | `hf.shock(x, y, power?)` | A pressure wave: a ring that bends the picture as it travels out from a point in the world. Three at a time. |
  | `hf.shake(k)` | Shakes the picture, 0 to 1, and dies away by itself. It happens in the post pass, so the HUD holds still and the scene's camera never moves: the bot's run is identical. |
  | `hf.flash(k)` | A sudden light the exposure was not ready for; it settles within the second. |

- **Shader sprites.** `hf.fx(kind, x, y, w, h)` gives an image drawn by a fragment shader of its own (`src/hifiFx.ts`), for the things no still drawing can be. A look places, turns, layers, tints and fades it like any image, and hands it up to eight numbers each step with `fxSet()`; its clock is the scene's. `plume` is a rocket exhaust: a pencil of flame with shock diamonds in thick air, swelling into a pale bell as the air thins, cut short by the ground at the pad; it burns kerosene orange or methane blue. `entry` is the shock layer of a re-entry: a bow standing off the blunt end, boiling, and streaming back up the track. `arc` is an electric arc from one end of the sprite to the other, struck again along a new path many times a second; tint it for the gas it burns in. Without WebGL `fx()` gives nothing and the look keeps its plain stand-in. To add a kind, write its `main()` in `FX`; each sprite is one draw call.
- **Fallbacks.** Without WebGL the layers, shadows, reflections and animation stay, and lighting and the post pass are skipped. Players choose SD, HD or XG graphics with `G` or the corner button (`hifiOn()` and `xgOn()` in `src/core.ts`, remembered; XG, which is HD plus [the 3D renderer](#the-3d-renderer), by default). The map and the title screen rebuild at once; inside a level the game asks whether to restart the level now, or keeps the new choice for the next level started). `?hifi=0` turns the layer off for one visit; `?renderer=canvas` forces the Canvas renderer to check the fallback.
- **Budget.** The look must hold the display's refresh rate at 1280 × 720: measure frame intervals over 20 s or more with the level running, and compare with `?hifi=0`.

## The 3D renderer

`src/gfx3d` is a renderer of its own for levels that want a real third dimension. It has its own WebGL2 context on its own canvas, laid exactly over Phaser's (`Descent.mount()`), with a second 2D canvas above it for the instruments. The Phaser scene stays in charge of everything that is not a picture: keys (`held`, `just`), the clock (`update`'s delta, so pause and game speed work unchanged), sound, `help()`, the bot and `finish()`. Once per shown frame (Phaser's `postrender` event) the scene hands the engine a `Frame`: camera, altitude, attitude, heat, parachutes, and so on.

- **No assets.** There is no mesh, model or image. Every pixel is ray-marched in `shaders.ts`: a planet of the Earth's size with a physical atmosphere (Rayleigh, Mie, ozone), a volumetric cloud layer shaped by a generated weather map, cirrus, a sea of summed wave trains with foam and sun glitter, and the vehicle, parachutes and ship as signed-distance geometry with physically based shading, soft shadows and reflections of the real sky and weather. Re-entry plasma, thruster gas and spray are volumes. Noise textures (a 96³ cloud volume, the weather map, a sheet for the sea) are computed on the graphics card at start-up.
- **Frame.** Scene at reduced size, jittered, into a half-float target (colour, and depth in alpha) → temporal anti-aliasing that also upsamples to the canvas → a seven-level bloom chain → lens (ghosts, streak, fringing, barrel), exposure, ACES tone curve, grade, grain.
- **Budget.** `Engine.adapt()` reads the graphics card's time per frame (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`) and moves the share of pixels that are ray-marched (`scale`, 0.4 to 1), then the step counts (`quality`), to hold about 11 ms. Without the timer the scale stays where it starts.
- **Units and precision.** Kilometres for the planet, metres for the vehicle, both measured from the camera or the vehicle, never from the planet's centre: `altOf()` and `shellCam()` are written so nothing is lost in the planet's radius.
- **Fallback.** `can3d()` is false without WebGL2 and float render targets. A level names another mechanic in `params.fallback`; `Descent` starts that scene instead, also when the player chose SD or HD. Graphics have three tiers (`G`, or the corner button; `?gfx=sd|hd|xg`): SD is the plain look, HD the hi-fi layer, XG is HD plus this renderer in the levels that have a 3D telling (`xgOn()` in `core.ts`). XG is the default where the browser can run it.
- **Music.** `score.ts` is a score written for the level and played live through the game's music bus (`musicOut()` in `audio.ts`): layers that the scene cross-fades by telling it the `Mood` and how hard the air is working.
- **Checking it.** `scene.shot()` returns the frame (world and instruments) as a JPEG data URL; `scene.settle = true` snaps the camera to its set-up. Set the scene's fields (`h`, `v`, `th`, `chute`, …) while it is paused to look at any moment of the fall.
- **What it can draw.** The Electron first stage, drogue and main parachutes (and the drogue trailing above the main one: `Frame.trail`), the recovery ship, and a helicopter with its rotors, line and hook (`Frame.heli`); `Descent.ts` and `Catch.ts` are the two scenes that use it.
- **A new 3D level** of the same kind is a `THEMES` entry and params (§10.19). A different vehicle or place means new functions in `shaders.ts` (`mapBody`, `shadeVeh`) chosen by a uniform.
- **The drive** has a program of its own, `DRIVE` (`src/gfx3d/drive.ts`): `SCENE`'s text with its own `main()`, so the planet, the air, the clouds, the sea's waves and the launch pad's shapes are the same code; it is compiled the first time the level asks (`Engine.drive()`), and `Frame.drive` selects it. The world is metres in the stretch's frame (x east, z south), from where the truck starts; the camera and the truck are passed as uniforms, and the temporal filter sees the world move past the truck as it sees the sea move past a falling stage. `src/drive/world.ts` lays each stretch out on the CPU in under a second, while the picture is faded out: the road as a 4 m table (`Track`, which is also the truck's physics), and textures over the stretch's box (1024², about 2 m a texel): distance from the road and along it, the ground's height with the road cut and filled into it (so the GPU and the truck's wheels agree), what covers the ground (water, bush, scrub, sand, town, paddock, cliff, the railway), a height map of what stands (for shadows and the shade under trees, marched like the hills), and a grid of 6 m cells listing every thing that stands in each (buildings, trees, poles, posts, signs, plants, wires), which the renderer walks along each ray. Nothing in this path may use `isnan`: on some GPUs it switches the whole program to strict floating point and doubles its cost.

## Cards and text

Story, intro, result, leaderboard and controls are DOM cards over the canvas (`showCard(html, buttons, photo?)` in `core.ts`), so long text, links and photos stay crisp and selectable. Enter confirms the first button. While a card is open, game input is off.

## Save, score and version

- **Save** is one `localStorage` object (`carbon-to-orbit-save`): stars per level, easter eggs found, secrets unlocked, people met, where the marker stands.
- **Game speed.** `+` and `−` change how many game steps each screen frame takes while a level is running (`speed()` in `core.ts`, applied by the loop wrapper in `main.ts`). Every step is still a normal 60 Hz step, so a scene needs no changes to support it.
- **Score** is derived from the save by `tally()` in `src/leaderboard.ts`, using the points in `leaderboard/src/rules.js`. `finish()` records each level's best points: stars × 100, scaled by the average speed of that run (`speedFactor`).
- **Version** comes from `package.json` (`VERSION`); its major.minor is the **season**. Scores are only comparable within a season, so bump the minor version whenever levels are added or points change.
- **Leaderboard** is optional. With `VITE_LEADERBOARD_URL` set, the card reads and writes a Cloudflare Worker ([leaderboard/README.md](../leaderboard/README.md)); `npm run dev` serves a stand-in at `/__lb`; with neither, the card shows only your own score.

## Rules the code enforces by convention

- **Fan project.** Official mission patches are in `src/assets/patches/`, listed with their mission and source page in `patches.json`; `patchImg()` shows them on a level's cards with a credit and link, and the launch scene puts the first one on the fairing. No other Rocket Lab logos or media are committed: official photos appear only from a player's own files in the git-ignored `src/assets/official/`. `?clean=1` leaves both out.
- **Facts carry sources.** Every level lists `sources`; text about real people states facts, never invented speech.
- **Peter Beck is the main character.** Another person leads a level through `hero`, or joins as a running mate through `params.buddy` (spec §7.0a). One level, High Tide, is led by an invented fan who is nobody real: the scene draws them itself and shows its own name plate, so `hero` and `CHARACTERS` are not involved. Two levels give the player a game role on the name plate instead of a person: the edit desk (Four channels, one call) and the fluids console (Chill Down, which has nobody in it at all).
